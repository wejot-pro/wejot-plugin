# Research-Question Quality

Use this reference during research definition.

## Reject pseudo-questions

- A broad direction without a real uncertainty, such as “research the needs of group X.”
- A question whose answer is already assumed or limited to familiar common sense.
- A closed yes/no formulation that hides the mechanism, boundary, or decision behind it.

## Require three properties

1. **A genuine puzzle:** an observation differs from expectation or creates a consequential information gap.
2. **Useful information:** the question carries enough background, boundary, and current judgment to guide inquiry.
3. **A contestable answer:** plausible evidence could change the hypothesis or the business decision.

## Decompose complex questions

Move from phenomenon to explanation, boundary, and decision:

1. What is happening, for whom, where, and when?
2. Which mechanisms or competing explanations might account for it?
3. Under which conditions does the pattern change or fail?
4. What decision would change for each plausible answer?

For every sub-question, mark whether structured self-report can answer it. Route exploratory “why/how” needs to interviews, observed behavior to behavioral data, causal claims to experiments or suitable quasi-experimental evidence, and contextual facts to secondary research when appropriate.
